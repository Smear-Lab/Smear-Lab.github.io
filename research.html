Active sampling in olfaction

A mouse does not receive odor, it takes it. Sniffing is the sampling behavior, and it is under the animal's control: rate, depth, and timing all change with what the animal is doing. This means the input to the olfactory system is partly authored by the animal itself.

We found that odor responses in mitral and tufted cells are locked to the sniff with a precision comparable to other sensory systems, and that mice can read that timing: shifting the phase of an optogenetic stimulus within the sniff cycle changes what the animal reports perceiving. Sniff phase is not an artifact to be regressed out. It is part of the code.

Olfactory bulb circuits during behavior

When a mouse is left alone to behave without a task or a stimulus, its breathing is not noise. It organizes into persistent rhythmic states, and population activity in the olfactory bulb moves between matching states. The same populations carry the animal's location well enough to decode position at accuracies comparable to hippocampal populations recorded under the same conditions.

This raises a measurement problem we also work on directly: the local field potential in the bulb is often used as a proxy for the breathing rhythm, and it is a less reliable proxy than the literature assumes.

Closed-loop behavior: the Clickbait paradigm

Most behavioral assays, including ones described as naturalistic, sample a narrow slice of what an animal does. The animal visits the same few locations and everything else gets treated as noise. If sampling precedes discerning, that ordering is backwards.

Clickbait is a video-tracked operant system that closes the loop on a freely moving mouse in real time, using a click as a conditioned reinforcer to incentivize uniform spatial exploration without introducing sensory confounds. Trained mice explore far more uniformly than controls, and mice carrying recording implants acquire the task, so the paradigm is usable for electrophysiology. A methods paper is in preparation.

Clicker training as a technique is owed to Marian Breland.

Observing rather than only quantifying: ViSoND

Dimensionality reduction is a decision about what to throw away, made before you have looked. ViSoND maps neural and behavioral timeseries into sound and image, MIDI-mapped and usable in an ordinary audio workstation, so that structure can be noticed by ear and eye before it has been summarized. It is deliberately lossy, and the losses are chosen to be different from the ones a summary statistic makes.
