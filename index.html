The Smear Lab active sensing and olfactory processing Institute of Neuroscience, University of Oregon We study active sensing: how animals move their bodies and sensory organs to gather information about the world. Our main system is olfaction, where sniffing is the sampling behavior and the olfactory bulb is the first place the brain processes what the nose picks up. We build closed-loop behavioral tools to study these processes in freely moving mice. The lab is run by Matt Smear in the Institute of Neuroscience at the University of Oregon. Research Active sampling in olfaction: how sniffing shapes what the brain receives, and how animals adjust sampling to the task in front of them. Olfactory bulb circuits: how the first stage of the olfactory system represents and transforms odor during behavior. Closed-loop behavior: the Clickbait paradigm, a video-tracked operant system that closes the loop on a freely moving mouse in real time. A methods paper is in preparation. Navigation by sound: how animals localize and move toward targets using auditory cues.
